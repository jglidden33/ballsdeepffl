Balls Deep Fantasy Football League · 2012–2025

Our
Story.

Fourteen years of championships, questionable draft picks, and the occasional roster shakeup — in our own words.

2012
Champion: Jason G.

Welcome to the debut of the Balls Deep Football League! Back then, we called ourselves the "Aggressive Football Football League," which is a mouthful, but we were just getting started. The original gang included Ben G, Jason G, Chris P, Daniel H, Ben F, Chad G, Tony M, and Cordel G. This year will forever be remembered for Benny's bold first overall pick of Cedric Benson—because why not? We also discovered the hard way that playing the last week of the NFL season as our championship week was a terrible idea. Lesson learned! Jason took home the first-ever championship title, edging Chris P. 125–104 in a final that followed a 77–77 tie in the quarterfinals—which Jason won on a tiebreaker.

2013
Champion: Chris B.

We officially rebranded to the Balls Deep Football League and expanded to 12 teams with Jeff H, Corey R, Chris B, and Jen G joining the fray. We switched things up with a 2-player keeper format. Jason, our reigning champ, crushed the regular season but stumbled in the playoffs, leaving room for the newbie Chris B to snatch the title from fellow rookie Jen G, 106.1–64.9.

2014
Champion: Cordel G.

Jen bid farewell, and we welcomed Scott R. We tried out a new division setup with 2 divisions of 6 teams each. Cordel G nabbed his first championship, narrowly beating Ben F, 94.25–91.95—still the smallest championship margin in league history—thanks to an untimely AJ Green injury that should have sealed the deal for Ben. Fantasy football heartbreak at its finest!

2015
Champion: Tony M.

We said goodbye to both Chris's and brought in Brad F and Jordan M. After experimenting with 2 divisions, we reverted to our trusty 4-division format. We also shook things up with an NFL draft style. Tony clinched his first championship, defeating Corey R in the final showdown, 131.1–109.55—the first of three finals Corey would go on to lose.

2016
Champion: Daniel H.

We lost Chad due to some "minor" allegations, making him a legendary cautionary tale. Enter Chris S, our third Chris, taking the 12th spot. Jason dominated the league all season (11-2, best record in the league) but was upset in the finals by Daniel H, 133.35–112.9, who claimed his first championship victory.

2017
Champion: Tony M. (2nd)

Jordan exited the league after two seasons of uninspired play, and Derrick T stepped in. Tony M made history by becoming the first two-time champion, capping a league-best 12-1 season with a 79.15–76.8 win over Corey R—the second-closest championship in league history, and Corey's second finals loss in three years. He couldn't catch a break.

2018
Champion: Jason G. (2nd)

Scott left to redirect his funds to the dancers at Dooks—solid life choice! JC P joined to fill the spot. Another owner claimed their second championship in consecutive years as Jason G defeated Ben G to take the title, 117.85–87.45.

2019
Champion: Jason G. (3rd)

We said goodbye to Tony M and Jeff H, and welcomed Luke S and the mystery manager known only as "Larry." Daniel, inspired by Benny's infamous Cedric Benson pick, one-upped him by selecting Tarik Cohen first overall. Cordel dominated the regular season but faltered in the playoffs, allowing Jason to grab his second straight and third overall championship, 142.75–101.8, much to Corey R's chagrin—Corey's lost all three finals he's reached!

2020
Champion: Jason G. (4th)

After some ribbing for last year's draft blunder, Daniel finished last and decided to call it quits. Travis J filled his spot. The league made a significant shift from standard scoring to Half-PPR, leading to higher scores across the board. Fueled by the new scoring, Jason G won his third consecutive championship, beating JC 156.6–112.3 in the finals—still the largest championship blowout in league history. Could we be witnessing a dynasty?

2021
Champion: Ben F.

Life got hectic, leading to Cordel's departure. Kaiden P joined us to fill the vacancy. Against all odds, Benny defied expectations and claimed the championship, defeating Travis J in the final, 124.15–105.05.

2022
Champion: Travis J.

This year was a rollercoaster! Over the offseason, we learned Ryan was leaving, and "Larry" wanted out but was willing to stay if needed. Unfortunately, our new recruit had second thoughts and bailed before the draft due to some drunken shenanigans. We almost reduced to 10 teams but decided to stick with 12. Ben G and Jason each took on a second team, creatively named the Alcoholics and the Stranglers. We returned to the snake draft and adjusted the keeper rule, bumping keepers up one round from the previous year. The season ended uniquely with the Damar Hamlin situation, but luckily, Travis had already secured his first championship over Jason, 106.39–97.15.

2023
Champion: Jason G. (5th)

Cordel rejoined, allowing the Alcoholics to sober up and exit the league, but the Stranglers stayed on. We updated the keeper rule to allow for 2 to 5 players. Jason, last year's runner-up, came back with a vengeance to take down JC, 124.83–113.73, earning his fifth overall championship—capping a 1,670.93-point season, the 5th-highest single-season total in league history.

2024
Champion: Cordel G. (2nd)

This year was pure chaos. Two new managers, Jacob C. and Brian O., joined the league, replacing Kaiden and the Stranglers, while the first overall pick (CMC) ghosted the league for half the season before limping off with a season-ending injury. This year's main lesson? Scoring a ton of points doesn't matter if you don't win your matchups—just ask Luke, who finished as the second-highest scorer but missed the playoffs, while JC, with the 10th-most points, landed 3rd place. In a finals that nobody had on their bingo card, Cordel took down Derrick to secure his second title, 132.23–122.81.

2025
Champion: Jacob C.

For the first time in years, the offseason came and went with the same twelve managers reporting for duty—no departures, no replacements. Turned out to be the year to do it: Ben G. put up the single highest-scoring regular season in league history (1,706.23 points), and JC P.—fresh off popping Bijan Robinson with the first overall pick—was right behind him at 1,676.46, the two best seasons anyone's ever had, in the same year. JC backed it up by ripping off an 11-0 start on his way to a 12-2 finish, tied for the most wins in a single season ever, and along the way buried Derrick T. 182.61–73.55 in Week 6, one of the five biggest blowouts in league history. Jason G. quietly outscored 2-seed Jacob C. by nearly 90 points over the season and still finished a game behind him in the standings—this league's favorite recurring lesson. Then the playoffs flipped the script entirely: 6-seed Cordel G. snuck in at a shaky 7-7, knocked off Jason in the quarterfinal, then blew out JC's historic 12-2 squad by 52 points in the semis, ending the top seed's dream season early. JC settled for third; Cordel advanced to face Jacob C., who was making just his second career playoff trip, and Jacob closed it out 121.86–105.66 to claim his first championship.
