New Season, New Rules

The draft is in the books. Rosters are set. Week 1 is close enough to taste.

In a league entering its fifteenth season, three rule changes are on the books that will shape how rosters get built for years to come — one live right now, one landing before the 2027 draft, one that rewrites the keeper rules entirely in 2028.

None of it touches what makes this league what it is. Fifteen years of the same core group, still finding new ways to make each other miserable every September. But the rules that keep it fair have to grow up too. Here's the rundown before Week 1.

Keeper Eligibility, Tightened

Starting this season, only players who were drafted and stayed on a roster 100% of the year can be kept for 2027. Touch the waiver wire once — claimed, added, dropped and re-added, by any team in the league — and that player is out of the keeper pool for good.
Effective 2026 Season

The draft-day-only rule

Traded players are the one exception. A straight trade never touches the waiver wire, so a player who changes hands via trade keeps his keeper eligibility intact — it's the swing through free agency that costs him.

What this means in practice: that league-winning waiver pickup at running back in Week 6 is great for this year's title run and dead on arrival for next year's keeper list. Keeper value now belongs to the managers who found the player on draft day, not whoever streams him off waivers in November.

The Draft Lottery Arrives

The season-long events that have always decided next year's draft order aren't going anywhere. What changes: instead of locking in draft slots directly, those results now feed into a draft lottery that sets the 2027 order.
Effective 2027 Draft

Same events, higher stakes

Think of it as raising the stakes rather than replacing the tradition. The events managers grind through all year still matter — they set the lottery odds. But there's now a layer of chance on lottery night, where every manager gets a shot at climbing the board, or a bad bounce that sends them back down it.

2028: The Keeper Overhaul

Beginning with the 2028 season, managers are capped at 1–3 keepers per team, and the keeper penalty doubles from one round to two. Round 1 and Round 2 picks become keeper-ineligible outright — there's no round left to bump them down to.
Effective 2028 Season

Fewer keepers, steeper cost

Under the new math, a player drafted in the 4th round carries a keeper value of the 2nd round the following year — not the 3rd, like today. The draft stays the best day on the fantasy calendar, not an afterthought bolted onto a roster that's already 80% set from keepers.

Why the League Is Tightening Up

Fifteen seasons in, the goal hasn't moved: keep the draft meaningful, and keep dynasties from calcifying into rosters nobody wants to compete against.

Locking keeper eligibility to draft-day picks rewards scouting and prep over waiver-wire luck. The lottery keeps the draft order compelling even after the standings are decided. The 2028 keeper squeeze keeps the draft the marquee event of the fantasy calendar.

“We're not trying to blow up what's worked for fifteen years — we're trying to make sure it still works for the next fifteen.” — The Commissioners

Opening Day

Season 15 Kickoff: Opening Day

The weight of Week 1.

In a league entering its fifteenth season, everyone understands the math: Week 1 counts the same as Week 7 or Week 12 in the standings.

But emotionally? Strategically? Historically? It carries far more weight.

An opening win does more than move a manager to 1–0. It builds confidence in roster construction. It validates draft-day conviction — and this year, draft-day conviction matters more than ever, since anything added off waivers is dead on arrival for next year's keeper list. Teams that start strong tend to operate from leverage rather than chasing it.

An early loss, on the other hand, exposes pressure points immediately. Depth questions surface. Bench decisions get scrutinized. Managers are forced to decide: stay patient, or pivot fast.

Week 1 isn't just the beginning of a 14-week regular season. It's the spark that ignites the championship chase — and, for the first time under the new rules, the first game of a keeper class that will actually stick.

The theory phase is over. Execution begins now.

The Ten Greatest Offenses in League History

Over fourteen seasons, thousands of fantasy points have been scored across Balls Deep FFL. Championships have been won, rivalries have risen and fallen, and countless Sundays have been decided by a single touchdown. But strip away the records and the rings, and one question remains: who actually built the best offense the league has ever seen?

#1 — Jason G. (2020) — 123.94 Points Per Game

The king remains on the throne. Jason G.'s 2020 squad didn't just win the league — it produced the single most dominant offensive season Balls Deep FFL has ever recorded, and then finished the job with the championship. Five years later, the closest anyone has come is Ben G.'s 2025 squad, still about two points per game short.

The House That Ben G. Built

No manager appears on this list more than Ben G. He owns three of the ten greatest offensive seasons ever assembled — 2025, 2021, and 2023 — more than anyone else in league history.

And yet: zero championships. Ben G.'s best finish in any of those three historic-scoring seasons is third place. It's the most productive offensive résumé in the league that has never once been cashed in for a ring.

  • Only 2 of the 10 greatest offensive seasons in league history ended in a championship — both belong to Jason G.
  • Ben G. owns 3 of the top 10 seasons and has never won it all.
  • Luke S. is the only other manager with multiple appearances on the list (2022, 2024) — also without a title in either season.
  • The current all-time record, 123.94 PPG, has stood since 2020 — but Ben G.'s 2025 season got within two points per game of it, the closest anyone has come.
A New Challenger Emerges

The newest entry on the leaderboard belongs to Ben G., whose 2025 squad averaged 121.87 points per game — the second-highest offensive season in league history. It wasn't enough. Ben G. fell in the semifinals to the eventual champion, Jacob C., who went on to win the 2025 title with a comparatively modest offense by this list's standards.

It's the same story that's followed Ben G. for three of his best seasons now: build the loudest offense in the league, and still watch someone else hold the trophy.

The best offense doesn't always win the championship — but somebody eventually has to break through with both.

Heading into Season 15, the question writes itself: will Ben G. finally turn a historic offense into a banner? Or will someone else's team be the one to finally chase down Jason G.'s 123.94-point ghost — and actually finish the job?

Before Week 1

Keeper eligibility, in plain English.

Drafted and rostered all year → keeper-eligible for 2027. Any waiver or free-agent touch, by anyone → permanently ineligible. Traded players keep their eligibility. Round 1 picks stay ineligible under the standard one-round penalty, same as always.

Keep this in mind all season.

Think twice before dropping a bench piece you might want to keep. A "buy-low" waiver claim is now a pure 2026 asset. Trades remain the only way to acquire a keeper-eligible player mid-season.

Season 15 is officially live. Six games, one Survivor casualty, and already a 75-point gap between the league's best score and its worst. Here's everything that happened in Week 1 — and everyone who should be a little embarrassed about it.

Cordel def. Jacob, 125.71–116.9. The reigning champion's title defense is off to a start best described as “fine.” Jacob put up a totally respectable 116.90 — respectable enough to lose to Cordel's 125.71. Nothing to see here, just the target on his back already doing its job.

Week 2 Recap

Season 15 · The Injury Report Crashes the Party

Week 2 sorted out the early pretenders from the real deal — Ben, Travis, and Luke are 2-0, three teams are already 0-2, and the Survivor pot lost its second member in as many weeks. But the story that'll follow this league into Week 3 didn't happen on a Balls Deep scoreboard at all: a brutal Sunday-and-Monday injury sweep across the NFL left three starting quarterbacks and a handful of skill players banged up, and this league did not escape unscathed. More on that below.

Week 2 Results

Jacob def. Derrick, 159.88–101.75. Josh Allen went nuclear for 43.3 points — the single best individual performance of the week in this league — and Travis Kelce tacked on 20.6 more to bury Derrick before halftime was a thought. Derrick got a respectable 20.6 out of Christian McCaffrey and it barely moved the needle.

Ben def. Bradley, 163.11–118.83. Back-to-back weeks atop the league — 138.12 in Week 1, now 163.11 — behind Jared Goff (33.05), Amon-Ra St. Brown (30.7), and a 26-point outing from the Carolina defense. Bradley's 118.83 would have won four of the six games this week. It lost by 44.

Jason def. Corey, 98.42–61.5. This one has an asterisk. Corey's Monday night was supposed to be Jaxson Dart's coming-out party against the Rams — instead Dart left with a sprained MCL before the first quarter ended, Jameis Winston did nothing with the reins, and both of Corey's starting wideouts got banged up in the same 24 hours (Mike Evans left with a hip injury, DJ Moore left with a shoulder injury of his own). The 61.5 is the low score of the week, and it comes with real questions heading into Week 3 — see the Injury Watch below.

Travis def. Benny, 103.46–95.32. Nothing flashy, just a 2-0 start built on being 8 points better on a Sunday. Benny's Davante Adams (35.5) was the best individual score on his roster all season and it still wasn't enough.

Luke def. JC, 103.61–87.39. Brock Purdy (31.35) and CeeDee Lamb (31.3) combined for more than 62 points on their own, and Luke didn't need much else to move to 2-0. JC is 0-2 and already staring up at the standings.

Brian def. Cordel, 79.74–76.92. The closest game of the week by a mile — 2.82 points — and it happened with Saquon Barkley playing on one leg. Barkley took a stinger on the Eagles' first offensive snap and finished with 9 total yards, but Tank Bigsby (13 carries, 33 yards, a TD) and Will Shipley (24 yards) did just enough in relief for Brian to survive by less than a field goal.

Week 2 Previews

One week in the books, eleven teams still alive in Survivor, and a full slate of chances to either build on Week 1 or make everyone forget it happened. Here's who's playing who.

Jacob (0-1) vs. Derrick (1-0). Jacob's title defense is one loss from officially becoming a bit. Derrick looked sharp enough in Week 1 to make this a real problem, not just a scheduling inconvenience.

Travis (1-0) vs. Benny (0-1). Travis is 1-0 and feeling good about it; Benny is 0-1 and should not feel good about anything, having watched Caleb Williams put up 39.95 points on his own bench last week.

Cordel (1-0) vs. Brian (0-1). Cordel's off to a strong start and gets a Brian squad still nursing a 1.77-point paper cut from Week 1. Sympathy is not on the schedule.

Bradley (1-0) vs. Ben (1-0). The only unbeaten-versus-unbeaten game on the board. Someone's 1-0 has to become someone's 1-1, and neither of them is going to enjoy finding out which.

Jason (0-1 · Eliminated) vs. Corey (0-1). The league's only guaranteed 0-2 game. Jason's already out of the Survivor money and Corey's not far behind him in the standings — whoever wins this gets to feel better about themselves for exactly one week.

Luke (1-0) vs. JC (0-1). Luke rides a Week 1 win into a matchup with a JC squad that never got out of the mid-100s. Nothing here suggests that changes.

Game of the Week

Bradley vs. Ben

The Only 1-0 vs. 1-0 Game on the Board

Bradley put up 135.30 in Week 1. Ben put up 138.12. Whoever wins this one is the only team standing 2-0 while owning one of the league's two best scores so far — and whoever loses gets a very fast reminder of how short a perfect record lasts.
